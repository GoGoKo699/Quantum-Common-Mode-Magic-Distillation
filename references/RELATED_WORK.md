# Related work

## Exact conversion and number fields

Universal concentration converts identical copies of an unknown pure nonstabilizer qubit into a fixed exact CCZ state. Rizzo and Leone prove a six-copy threshold and use stabilizer-orthogonal projections in the symmetric subspace. Their result and their discussion of catalytic $`T`$ conversion distinguish exact CCZ production from direct, uncatalyzed $`T`$ production. In the present source model, each delivered carrier has a specified sensitivity to the unknown phase. Different sensitivities can create a weighted charge sector containing both even and odd nominal weights. The [exact extraction theorem](../science/exact-extraction.md) then gives direct $`T`$ production and classifies the complete gain list. [Rizzo and Leone, arXiv:2608.13376v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13376v2)

Beverland, Campbell, Howard and Kliuchnikov use a number-field invariant of stabilizer processing to rule out certain exact conversions, including conversion from finitely many CCZ states to a $`T`$ state. The [sector converse](../science/exact-extraction.md#converse-for-adaptive-circuits) applies the Gaussian-rational restriction after separating the unknown phase frequencies. On this family of inputs, an explicit two-string parity projection supplies the matching constructive criterion. The [yield bound](../science/yield-and-robustness.md#pointwise-yield-bound) uses the same field extension to compare the two parity components of each sector. [Beverland et al., *Quantum Science and Technology* **5**, 035009 (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01124)

Kwan studies the magic density, formation cost and extraction yield of Dicke states. A homogeneous phase response gives sectors of fixed Hamming weight, and each normalized sector is a Dicke state up to phase. Kwan shows a number-field obstruction to uncatalyzed exact $`T`$ extraction from a Dicke state and develops extensive average yields for CCZ and catalytic $`T`$ extraction. With heterogeneous gains the sectors instead come from weighted charges, allowing a single sector to mix weight parities. [Kwan, arXiv:2611.17623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17623)

## Correlation and source control

Wei and colleagues show that separable states can hide magic from local marginals while allowing it to be recovered with stabilizer processing and classical communication. This establishes the general possibility of magic in correlations without entanglement. The [two-mode example](../science/three-input-protocol.md#how-the-coherence-can-evade-two-qubit-tomography) is a particular source with unknown additive phases and all proper marginals maximally mixed. For that source, extraction works phase by phase at a sharp probability. The criterion and converse treat a delivered family of noisy inputs with fixed sensitivities and no classical key for the unknown phases. [Wei et al., arXiv:2608.25950v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.25950v1)

Wang and colleagues relate correlations of locally Clifford-twirled inputs to the acceptance and polarization of a five-to-one magic-state distillation code. Their protocol resolves gain and loss arising from higher-order moments, including cases in which one- and two-qubit data agree. The protocol here uses the coherent phase relation of the untwirled input and its output guarantee is exact with unknown drift throughout the specified family. [Wang et al., arXiv:2610.36963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36963)

Erew and colleagues use composite pulses to suppress systematic preparation errors in the source, including Rabi errors and detuning in driven systems. Their access to coherent source pulses is essential to that approach. The [delivered-state task](../science/model.md) starts after preparation and offers only known sensitivity coefficients and the raw states to the receiver. When extra coherent control or a clean reference is available, it must be assessed as part of a different source-control comparison. [Erew et al., *Physical Review A* **114**, 022415 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1103/ngrr-863r)

## Background

The stabilizer description, syndrome projection and Clifford decoding are standard. Gottesman develops these tools and their use for fault-tolerant ancillas. Bravyi and Kitaev develop the resource role and distillation of magic states with ideal Clifford operations. Bartlett, Rudolph and Spekkens explain how phase averaging preserves coherence inside fixed charge sectors. [Gottesman (2009)](https://arxiv.org/html/0904.2557v1) · [Bravyi and Kitaev (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.71.022316) · [Bartlett, Rudolph and Spekkens (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.79.555)

## Source bursts

Balada Gaggioli and Mareček reduce ordinary distillation to stabilizer code projections and use field conjugation to relate complementary fault patterns. Proposition 5 gives the minimum of nine independently faulty equatorial T inputs for quadratic suppression. In the [mixed phase-and-burst model](../science/burst-converse.md), continuous charge separation makes that result applicable to at most five logical T inputs within any independent-phase sector. The same separation and a stabilizer-code support argument yield a direct fifth-order obstruction. [Balada Gaggioli and Mareček (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31115v1)
